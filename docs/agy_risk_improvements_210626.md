## 1. Overview of the `ai-saham` Risk Concept

The core risk engine within `ai-saham` is designed to be a deterministic-first, offline-capable analysis framework. It converts technical market indicators into qualitative risk assessments rather than generating raw buy/sell trading signals. 

### A. Evaluated Inputs
The risk engine maps the latest state of a stock using:
*   **Momentum (RSI):** Evaluates overbought or oversold conditions.
*   **Trend (EMA/SMA Divergence):** Measures the absolute percentage difference and relative crossover direction of the 20-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) and Simple Moving Average (SMA).

### B. Standard Risk Profiles
*   **Conservative:** Strict thresholds (RSI > 75 or < 25; EMA/SMA divergence >= 1%). Requires **both** indicators to agree (e.g., oversold + uptrend) to signal a non-moderate risk level.
*   **Balanced:** Standard thresholds (RSI > 70 or < 30; any EMA/SMA divergence). Utilizes **majority rule** logic.
*   **Aggressive:** High sensitivity thresholds (RSI > 60 or < 40; EMA/SMA divergence >= 0.1%). Either indicator alone can trigger a signal, with RSI taking priority.

## 2. Academic & Empirical Validation (IDX/IHSG)

Academic and empirical research on the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX/IHSG) provides crucial context on the effectiveness and limits of this technical-based approach:

1.  **Technical Indicator Validity in emerging markets:**
    *   *Finding:* Local academic studies (e.g., on LQ45 and IDX30 constituents) show that RSI and Moving Averages provide statistically significant indicators of momentum and trend shifts.
    *   *Signal Confluence:* Research demonstrates that combining momentum oscillators (like RSI) with trend-following metrics (like moving averages) significantly reduces false crossovers compared to using them in isolation.
    *   *Friction Costs:* Studies warn that high-frequency trading based purely on short-term technical indicators can have its returns eroded by IDX transaction fees (typically `0.15% - 0.35%` per round trip in Indonesia).
2.  **Foreign Flows ("Bandarmology") Efficacy:**
    *   *Finding:* Research confirms a strong, bidirectional correlation between foreign transaction volume/ownership changes and JCI (Jakarta Composite Index) returns. Foreign inflows act as a leading sentiment driver for large-cap stocks.
    *   *Global Macro Drivers:* Foreign capital flows in Indonesia are highly sensitive to global macro variables, specifically the **US Dollar Index (DXY)** and **US Federal Reserve interest rates**. High global rates lead to systematic capital flight from the IDX.

## 3. IHSG Microstructure: Strengths & Gaps

### Strengths of the Current Model
*   **Deterministic Integrity:** The rule engine is written entirely in deterministic Python, ensuring transparency and repeatability.
*   **AI Isolation:** AI is restricted to a read-only role (`ExplainRiskUseCase`), generating human-friendly interpretations without altering the raw risk level. This ensures compliance with auditability standards.
*   **Regime Pre-filtering:** Assessing individual stock setups inside the broader IHSG market regime (Bullish/Bearish/Sideways) helps filter out high-risk trades during systemic downturns.

### Structural Gaps & Risks
*   **The Liquidity Trap:** Many mid-cap and small-cap stocks on the IDX are highly illiquid. Static risk rules (e.g., RSI < 30) do not account for bid-ask spreads or fake bid-walls (*Jemuran*). Buying an illiquid technical signal runs the risk of severe slippage during entry/exit.
*   **Sector Volatility and Cyclicals:** IHSG is heavily weighted by banking and commodity cyclical stocks (e.g., Coal: ADRO, ITMG; Metals: ANTM). Static thresholds are inefficient here; commodity stocks can remain highly oversold or overbought for months depending on global commodity pricing cycles.

## 4. Strategic Recommendations for Code Alignment

To enhance the accuracy and robustness of the `ai-saham` risk engine, the following structural improvements are proposed for future development cycles:

### Recommendation 1: Implement Adaptive Regime Tuning (ART)
*   **Target:** Application / Domain Policy
*   **Detail:** Transition the static thresholds in `src/domain/rules/` to check an adaptive override configuration (`regime_overrides.yaml`). Dynamic thresholds should be generated by analyzing win/loss attributions across different market regimes and sector cycles.

### Recommendation 2: Introduce a Liquidity Filter
*   **Target:** Application Use Case (`AssessRiskUseCase`)
*   **Detail:** Establish minimum liquidity gates. The system should calculate the median daily transaction value (e.g., minimum IDR 5 Billion) over a trailing 20-day window. If a stock falls below this, the risk engine should automatically flag it with a supplementary warning or classify it as `HIGH_RISK` due to liquidity constraints.

### Recommendation 3: Integrate Macro Signals in JCI Regime Detection
*   **Target:** Infrastructure / Application Use Case
*   **Detail:** Fetch and correlate macro indicators (like the **DXY** and **USD/IDR exchange rate**). If DXY is in a strong daily uptrend, the system should trigger a "systemic risk-off" regime override, automatically tightening the entry requirements across all technical profiles.

## 5. DB-Contextual Recommendations (Leveraging Existing Schema)

To maximize the value of the local data already collected in `data.db`, the risk engine should be enhanced to cross-reference technical price signals with fundamental, transaction, and ownership data tables.

### Recommendation 4: Piotroski F-Score Safeguard (Value Trap Prevention)
*   **Target:** Domain Policy / Application Use Case
*   **Database Source:** `company_fundamentals.piotroski_f_score`
*   **Detail:** Prevent technical "buy setups" on companies in financial distress. If a stock triggers a technical `LOW_RISK` signal, but its `piotroski_f_score <= 3` (indicating weak fundamentals, profitability, or solvency), the engine should override the assessment to `MODERATE` or `HIGH_RISK` and append a fundamental health warning to the rationale.

### Recommendation 5: Bandarmology Divergence Filter
*   **Target:** Domain Rules / Application Use Case
*   **Database Source:** `bandar_detector` (`five_day_accdist`, `top3_accdist`, `total_buyer`, `total_seller`)
*   **Detail:** In the IHSG, local institutional accumulation/distribution (Bandarmology) heavily determines technical breakout success. If a stock triggers a technical `LOW_RISK` or uptrend signal, but the `five_day_accdist` is in heavy distribution (net negative), the risk level should be downgraded to reflect the risk of retail panic or institutional dumping.

### Recommendation 6: Dynamic Liquidity & Size Gates
*   **Target:** Application Use Case
*   **Database Source:** `company_fundamentals.market_cap_idr` & `candles.volume`
*   **Detail:** Establish risk modifiers based on market cap and liquidity to guard against manipulation. If a stock’s `market_cap_idr < 1_000_000_000_000` (IDR 1T / third-liner) or trailing 20-day median transaction volume is low, cap the maximum signal confidence score or automatically elevate the risk level to account for high spread slippage.

### Recommendation 7: Ownership Stability Filter
*   **Target:** Domain Policy
*   **Database Source:** `shareholding_composition` (`institution_pct`, `individual_pct`)
*   **Detail:** Incorporate ownership structure stability. High retail ownership (`individual_pct > 70%`) indicates high vulnerability to sentiment-driven panic selling. The risk engine should adjust the confidence score downward or default risk level upward when evaluating retail-dominated stocks, while increasing signal confidence for institution-backed stocks.

## 6. Institutional Architecture & Factor Standardization

To align the `ai-saham` risk engine with institutional models (such as Bloomberg's PORT MAC3 or MSCI Barra), the system architecture should evolve beyond static heuristic rules toward factor standardization and formal model separation.

### Recommendation 8: Transition from Static Rules to Z-Scoring
*   **Target:** Domain Primitives & Rules
*   **Detail:** Replace hardcoded indicator limits (e.g., `RSI < 25`) with relative Z-scores normalized against each ticker's own historical 3-year distribution:
    $$Z = \frac{x_t - \mu}{\sigma}$$
    where $x_t$ is the current indicator snapshot value, and $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the trailing mean and standard deviation for that specific stock. This automatically calibrates thresholds for sectoral variations (e.g., high volatility commodity stocks vs. low volatility blue-chip banks).

### Recommendation 9: Factor Decorrelation & Collinearity Filtering
*   **Target:** Domain Policy & Rule Engine
*   **Detail:** Prevent the risk engine from double-counting redundant risks. When combining multiple technical or transaction indicators, the engine should enforce factor decorrelation rules (e.g., preventing the simultaneous inclusion of multiple highly correlated price-momentum oscillators like RSI, Stochastic, and MACD).

### Recommendation 10: Structural Separation of Alpha vs. Risk Models
*   **Target:** Application Layer Architecture
*   **Detail:** Ensure a clean operational boundary in the code between signal generation (Alpha) and capacity constraints (Risk). The application use cases should compute technical trend signals (Alpha) independently, and then pass them through the Risk Engine to apply liquidity, capital exposure, and macro-regime constraints before execution.

## 7. Operational Design: Hierarchical Multi-Asset Risk Gating

Combining technical indicators with non-technical parameters (fundamentals, flows, macro) requires aligning different time horizons (daily momentum vs. quarterly fundamentals) to prevent lagging data from neutralizing active price signals. 

To achieve this, the application should process risk evaluation in a **hierarchical pipeline** rather than a flat mathematical formula:

```
[ Raw Data Feed ]
       │
       ▼
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Tier 1: Structural Gate (Fundamental)   │  <-- Evaluates PE, PBV, Piotroski F-Score
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │
       ├─► (FAIL: distress/value trap) ──► [ Automatic HIGH_RISK Assessment ]
       │
       ▼ (PASS)
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   Tier 2: Systemic Gate (Macro/Regime)   │  <-- Evaluates DXY, JCI Trend, Sector cycles
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │
       ▼ (Adapts Rule Thresholds)
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│    Tier 3: Execution Gate (Tech/Flows)   │  <-- Evaluates RSI & Bandar Concentration
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │
       ▼
[ Final RiskAssessment Output ]
```

### Recommendation 11: Establish Hierarchical Rule Evaluation
*   **Target:** Application Use Case (`AssessRiskUseCase`)
*   **Detail:** Implement a layered validation structure:
    1.  **Structural Gate:** Evaluated first as a binary pass/fail filter. If fundamentals are distressed (e.g., F-score $\le 3$), terminate the calculation immediately and return a `HIGH_RISK` assessment.
    2.  **Systemic Gate:** Evaluated second. If systemic trends (JCI Bearish or DXY spikes) are active, dynamically adjust the thresholds of the downstream execution indicators (e.g., tighten the RSI oversold threshold).
    3.  **Execution Gate:** Evaluated last, combining highly dynamic daily price indicators (RSI, MA) with transaction flows (net foreign buying, bandar accumulation).

### Recommendation 12: Maintain Backtesting Temporal Integrity
*   **Target:** Application Backtesting Framework
*   **Detail:** When combining technicals and fundamentals during historical backtests, the database query logic must restrict fundamental statement access to only the reports published *on or before* the technical snapshot date. This prevents "look-ahead bias" where the model inadvertently learns from future earnings releases.

## 8. Screener & Analyzer Integration Considerations

Integrating a multi-factor risk engine with the candidate screening system (`saham screen`) and analysis pipeline (`saham analyze`) requires resolving several structural and performance constraints.

### Recommendation 13: Relocate the Market Cap Floor Check (Early Pruning)
*   **Target:** Application Use Case (`AccumulationScreenUseCase`)
*   **Detail:** In [accumulation_screen.py](file:///Users/satriyo/dev/ai-saham/src/application/use_case/accumulation_screen.py), move the `market_cap_floor` verification to the very beginning of the `_evaluate_ticker` loop. Currently, the check is evaluated after querying seasonality, insider, analyst, and shareholding data. Skipping penny stocks before enrichment will eliminate substantial unnecessary database queries for non-viable candidates.

### Recommendation 14: Adopt a Post-Screening Risk Gating Funnel
*   **Target:** Application Layer Workflow
*   **Detail:** To preserve screening performance, avoid running the full `AssessRiskUseCase` on all 800+ index tickers during the initial scan. Instead, run the screener's technical and foreign flow rules to isolate the top candidates (e.g., 20–30 survivors). Run the risk engine calculations (Z-scoring, F-score checks) only on these survivors. Filter out any candidate flagged as `HIGH_RISK` before rendering the final CLI results.

### Recommendation 15: Share Data Snapshots to Deduplicate Queries
*   **Target:** Domain Interfaces / Use Cases
*   **Detail:** Modify the risk engine signature to accept pre-loaded data models. If the screener has already queried the fundamentals and bandar snapshots, pass these objects directly into the risk evaluation pipeline rather than letting the risk engine perform duplicate database calls.

### Recommendation 16: Extend Date-Bound Queries on Persistence Ports
*   **Target:** Infrastructure Layer / Persistence Ports
*   **Detail:** Modify the database provider ports (for fundamentals and shareholding composition) to accept an optional `date` filter parameter. By default, these interfaces pull the latest snapshot; adding date-bound constraints is necessary to prevent look-ahead bias during historical screen backtests.
